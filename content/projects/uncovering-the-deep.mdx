## A game that promotes marine conservation

Created for our undergrad thesis in Game Development, *Uncovering the Deep* is an educational game for children and the youth to learn hands-on through play. Our main goal is to spread awareness about protecting our local ocean ecosystems, and show how **overfishing and plastic waste** affect the creatures that live there.

Team: Julia Avesta, Sean Austin Julag-Ay, John Cedrick Austria. Published and free on itch.io (Windows).

## How this project came to be

Grounded in our thesis themes of education and environmental sustainability, we found key insights on marine life and the oceans that opened up an opportunity.

<StatCallout value="8M tons" label="of trash created during the pandemic, 25,000 tons reaching the ocean" />
<StatCallout value="80%+" label="of the ocean is still undiscovered" />
<StatCallout value="Billions" label="of fish lost to overfishing and bycatch" />

<CalloutBox type="insight">
The pandemic kept kids indoors. So we wanted to create a **casual, free game** that brings the ocean to them and builds urgency to protect it. Our earlier game, *Lost in Space*, inspired the proof of concept: we reused its exploration and item-collection mechanics.
</CalloutBox>

## What research told us, and what we designed

| What we found | What we designed |
| --- | --- |
| Children learn best from experience (Hilton, 2021) | Animals talk to Olive, so players see the trash from the animals' side |
| Real-world grounding builds trust (Beyond Blue, made with marine biologists) | An almanac pairing each sprite with a real photo of the animal |
| A good story carries the message (Mitscheck et al., 2017) | Olive, a relatable marine biologist protecting her own home |

## 3 ways to play

1. **Story Mode: Restoration.** Dive → collect trash / free animals → swim up to the submarine. Clear trash and free trapped animals.
2. **Story Mode: Exploration.** Meet animals → chat & trivia quiz → the animal joins your aquarium.
3. **Endless Mode: Ocean Cleanup.** Collect trash → swim into a whirlpool → new random map. Most trash before your oxygen runs out.

## Alpha testing with 54 people

Over two days, 48 students (a sixth-grade class at Sta. Cruz Elementary School and 21 DLSU-D students), 5 educators and 1 parent played the demo. All 54 answered a 27-question survey, rating each item from 1 to 5 against the **ISO/IEC 25010** quality model.

## Testing showed where play felt rough

<CalloutBox type="problem" title="Olive was harder to steer than expected">
Ease of control was the lowest-rated item at 4.3 out of 5. The weighty swim was intentional, but 13% were neutral and 1.9% disagreed.
</CalloutBox>

<CalloutBox type="problem" title="Hauling trash bags back felt like a chore">
Each bag slows Olive down, and reaching the spawn point with a full stack was tough, especially for older players.
</CalloutBox>

<CalloutBox type="problem" title="New players had no space to practice">
Testers asked for playable tutorial levels before Story Mode. Some were unsure of the controls without the tutorial panel.
</CalloutBox>

## Learning through play worked

Players enjoyed *Uncovering the Deep* and came away caring about the ocean's creatures, and educators saw its promise as a classroom tool.

## What we would build with more time

- **English and Filipino:** a language toggle so players who don't read English well can follow Story Mode.
- **Localizing levels:** local Filipino species and waters, so kids learn about the wildlife and plastic crisis around them.
- **Power-ups & cosmetics:** new ways to finish levels, plus wearable rewards that make replaying more fun.
- **A mobile version:** to increase accessibility so more kids can learn from the game.
- **Livening up the ocean:** more animals swimming by, moving kelp and coral, and voice-over for struggling readers.
- **Bosses and multiplayer:** boss levels, more characters and co-op play for kids who love playing together.
